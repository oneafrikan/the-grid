# Audit: zone-by-zone walkthrough

Walk the page in scroll order. A missing zone can itself be a finding (no proof, no FAQ). Conversion type changes what counts as the "ask": lead or enquiry form (a message sent), self-serve signup (an account created), direct purchase (a payment made).

## Two passes

- **Pass A, first visit (System 1):** the 5-second impression, then one scroll as the visitor, narrating honest reactions. Note where attention goes, where doubt appears, where motivation spikes or dies.
- **Pass B, principle sweep (System 2):** zone by zone, with the relevant `principles-*.md` open. Look for principles **used well** (say so), **violated** (friction), and **missing** (opportunity). Aim for 8-15 substantive findings.

## 0. Before the page

- Source and promise: what did the ad, email, search result or link promise? (Priming, Expectations Bias)
- Title tag and meta description match the H1? Mismatch is broken message match.

## 1. Above the fold

A first-time visitor should answer, within 5 seconds:

1. What is this? Concrete category, not slogan. (Tesler's Law, Curse of Knowledge)
2. Who is it for? Do I self-identify? (Barnum-Forer)
3. What do I get? Outcome, not features.
4. What do I do next? One visible, specific action. (Hick's Law, External Trigger, Von Restorff)

Also: is the H1 dominant, does the hero image add meaning (Picture Superiority), nav leaking paid traffic, first number shown (Anchoring), action-label specificity, mobile fold.

## 2. Problem and tension

- Names the pain in the visitor's own words? (Confirmation Bias, Internal Trigger, Empathy Gap)
- Agitates without insulting current choices? (Backfire Effect, Reactance)
- Creates tension the offer resolves? (Cognitive Dissonance, Curiosity Gap)

## 3. Offer and solution

- Value shown, not claimed: demo, screenshots, sample, before/after. (Aha! Moment, Labor Illusion)
- Features chunked into 3-5 themes. (Miller's Law, Chunking)
- Offer is one complete unit, extras grouped. (Unit Bias, Group Attractiveness)
- Jargon check; does simplification tip into vagueness? (Cognitive Load, Tesler's Law)

## 4. Proof

- Mix present: testimonials, logos, numbers, case studies, credentials, press. (Social Proof, Authority Bias, Halo)
- Specific beats generic: named person, role, result. One deep story beats ten quotes. (Singularity, Storytelling)
- Dated and current. (Availability Heuristic)
- Placed next to the claims and actions it supports. (Law of Proximity)
- Outliers flagged honestly. (Survivorship Bias, Hindsight Bias)

## 5. Price and commitment (purchase and paid signup)

- Anchor before price: value stack, comparison, cost of inaction. (Anchoring)
- Frame: per-period equivalents, versus the alternative. (Framing, Cashless Effect)
- Tiers: target plan centred and highlighted, fair default, honest decoy. (Decoy, Centre-Stage, Default Bias, Nudge)
- Risk reversal beside the buy button: guarantee, trial, cancel terms. (Loss Aversion, Proximity)
- Is the price findable? Hidden pricing costs trust. (Discoverability)
- Urgency real or flagged. (Scarcity, Parkinson's Law, Second-Order Effect)

## 6. Action and form (all three types)

- First ask sized to readiness; a smaller step for the not-ready. (Spark, Commitment & Consistency)
- Action label names the outcome ("Get my quote", "Start free", "Buy now"). (External Trigger)
- Biggest-contrast element in view, thumb-reachable, repeated or sticky on long pages. (Fitts, Von Restorff, Contrast)
- Lead forms: minimum viable fields, response-time promise. Signup: fewest fields, no card unless needed. Purchase: guest checkout, total cost visible. (Cognitive Load, Goal Gradient, Feedback Loop)
- "What happens next" stated beside the action. (Feedforward)
- Offer or price restated where the action happens. (Recognition Over Recall)

## 7. Objections and FAQ

- Top 3 real objections answered (time, money, "will it work for me", privacy). (Planning Fallacy, Loss Aversion)
- Known concerns addressed, not hidden. (Streisand Effect)

## 8. Ending

- Peak and last impression: final action plus reassurance, or a trail into footer links? (Peak-End, Serial Position)
- Exit path for the not-ready: email capture, free resource, reminder. (Provide Exit Points, Investment Loops, Zeigarnik)

## 9. Global sweep

- **Negativity sweep:** typos, broken images or links, placeholder text, mixed naming. Any one is an automatic high or medium finding. (Negativity Bias, Halo, Aesthetic-Usability)
- **Consistency:** primary actions share style and verb. (Law of Similarity)
- **Length and flow:** does each section earn the next scroll? (Flow, Curiosity Gap, Pareto)
- **Dark-pattern scan:** pre-ticked boxes, fake scarcity, confirmshaming, hidden costs, trick wording. Flag every one, whatever its conversion upside.
- **Mobile:** fold content, tap targets, font size, sticky action (only with screenshots or a browser).
