# Principles: Memory (17)

What people retain, and how an experience ends. Use for page or flow endings, delight, onboarding, retention and re-engagement. Definitions are our own wording.

| Principle | Meaning | Check (page, flow or test) |
|---|---|---|
| Provide Exit Points | A graceful way out at the right moment leaves a better memory than trailing off. | Ends with next steps, not a dead end; an exit path for visitors who are not ready. |
| Peak-End Rule | An experience is judged by its high point and its ending. | Name the peak and the last screen. Strong close, or legal links? |
| Sensory Appeal | Engaging more than one sense deepens the experience. | Video, audio, motion or tactile feedback vs a wall of text. |
| Zeigarnik Effect | Unfinished tasks nag at memory. | Started-not-finished states (profile 70% done, saved basket) prompting return. |
| Endowment Effect | Owning something, even briefly, raises its value. | Trials, samples, "your" workspace wording, saved configurations. |
| Chunking | Grouping items into units makes them memorable. | Content, steps and numbers grouped (modules, stages, threes). |
| Delighters | Unexpected pleasures are remembered and shared. | One tasteful surprise: witty microcopy, a small animation. |
| Internal Trigger | Feelings and situations cue behaviour without any prompt. | Is the product tied to a recurring moment or pain, so it is recalled unprompted? |
| Picture Superiority Effect | Images are remembered better than words. | Screenshots, diagrams or icons backing major claims. |
| Method of Loci | Place anchors memory. | Consistent layout and landmarks people can find their way back to. |
| Shaping | Behaviour is built by rewarding successive small steps. | Nurture path (page, resource, trial, paid); onboarding that ramps in stages. |
| Recognition Over Recall | Seeing is easier than remembering. | Offer, price and choices restated at the point of action. |
| Storytelling Effect | Narrative sticks better than facts. | A person, a problem and an outcome, or only a claim list? |
| Negativity Bias | Bad experiences weigh more and last longer than good ones. | Typos, broken links, dead images, harsh errors: one outweighs many positives. |
| Availability Heuristic | What comes to mind easily is judged likely or important. | Recent, dated, vivid proof over stale results. |
| Spacing Effect | Repeated exposure over time beats one cram. | Email or onboarding sequences vs a single dump. |
| Serial Position Effect | First and last items are remembered best. | Strongest point first and last in lists, steps, plan rows, testimonials. |
