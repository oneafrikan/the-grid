# Principles: Time (28)

Decision dynamics, motivation and commitment. Use for pricing, forms, urgency, defaults, friction and onboarding. Definitions are our own wording.

| Principle | Meaning | Check (page, flow or test) |
|---|---|---|
| Labor Illusion | Visible effort raises perceived value. | Show the work: "comparing 40 options...", process steps, research depth. |
| Default Bias | People stick with pre-set options. | Is the default the fair choice? Pre-ticked extras are a dark pattern. |
| Investment Loops | Putting something in gives reasons to return. | Saved progress, profiles, preferences, waitlists. |
| Loss Aversion | A loss hurts more than an equal gain pleases. | Honest cost of inaction; guarantee or free cancellation removing risk. |
| Commitment & Consistency | People act in line with what they have already said or done. | A small yes (quiz, checkbox, email) before the big ask. |
| Sunk Cost Effect | Prior investment makes quitting feel wasteful. | Long forms and onboarding show progress. Never exploit it. |
| Reactance | Feeling pushed makes people push back. | Pushy popups, autoplay sound, forced account creation, confirmshaming. |
| Law of the Instrument | With a hammer, everything looks like a nail. | Claims to solve everything; one tactic applied everywhere. |
| Temptation Bundling | Pairing a chore with a pleasure makes it easier. | A needed step paired with an immediate perk. |
| Dunning-Kruger Effect | Novices overrate their skill. | Copy that assumes accurate self-assessment; meet people where they think they are. |
| Discoverability | People can only use what they can find. | Price, terms, contact, key features findable? Hidden pricing costs trust. |
| Second-Order Effect | Actions have consequences of consequences. | Tactics that convert today but raise refunds, churn or distrust later. |
| Decision Fatigue | Decision quality falls as decisions pile up. | Count choices before the main action; simplify late steps. |
| Observer-Expectancy Effect | Researchers' expectations leak into results. | Tests and research: unbiased set-up, no leading questions. |
| Weber's Law | People notice change in proportion to the starting size. | Phase big changes; make discounts large enough to register. |
| Parkinson's Law | Work expands to fill the time available. | Real deadlines on offers or tasks; open-ended timelines stall. |
| Affect Heuristic | Current feeling steers judgement. | What emotion do imagery and copy trigger: fear, hope, boredom? |
| Hyperbolic Discounting | Rewards now beat bigger rewards later. | Immediate value ("first result in 2 minutes") vs distant payoff. |
| Chronoception | Time is perceived, not measured. | Does the process feel short? Waits filled; "takes 2 minutes" true? |
| Cashless Effect | Abstract payment hurts less than cash. | Wallets, credits or instalments: keep the real total clear. |
| Self-Serving Bias | We credit ourselves for wins and blame circumstances for failure. | Testimonials letting users own the win; errors worded as the system's fault. |
| Pareto Principle | Roughly 80% of effect comes from 20% of causes. | Which fifth of the page or flow does most of the persuading? Protect it. |
| Backfire Effect | Challenged beliefs can harden. | Don't attack the current approach; reframe it. |
| False Consensus Effect | People assume others share their views. | Copy assuming shared opinions; a team assuming users think like them. |
| Bandwagon Effect | Adoption attracts more adoption. | Momentum proof: recent sign-ups, usage counts, live activity (real only). |
| Barnum-Forer Effect | Vague statements feel personally accurate. | Generic "perfect for you" vs specific naming of who it is for. |
| IKEA Effect | Things we help build are valued more. | Configurators, builders, customisation steps. |
| Planning Fallacy | People underestimate how long tasks take. | Honest time estimates; reassurance about effort. |
